\subsubsection{What the fitted estimates and posterior graphs show}
For jute, the unrestricted MLE reliability is about 0.7526 and the unrestricted posterior mean is about 0.6656. The Bayesian estimate averages reliability over a posterior distribution rather than evaluating reliability at posterior mean rates. The difference between MLE and Bayes here reflects both the prior and the remaining uncertainty after censoring and record selection.

The ordered jute posterior mean is about 0.6989. This shift is partly imposed by excluding reliability below one half. In the carbon example the contrast is larger: the unrestricted and ordered Bayesian means are about 0.3623 and 0.5653. A change that large should be reported as sensitivity to an assumption, rather than as automatic improvement.

\pairplot{08_real_reliability_intervals_panel_01.png}{08_real_reliability_intervals_panel_02.png}{Jute posterior reliability histograms and the four point/interval estimates. Histogram draws come from the fitted posterior; interval types are identified in the text.}{fig:jute-posterior}
The intervals are wide because only a small part of the original sample enters the censored/record likelihood. In particular, six stress records contain less information than all thirty comparison measurements. The parameter posterior plots show skewness in the rates; this is one reason to retain full uncertainty rather than report only a point estimate.
